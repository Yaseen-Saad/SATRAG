- "Text Structure and Purpose": Analyze how a passage is organized, what the author's purpose is, or how a specific sentence/paragraph contributes to the overall text. The passage may describe a process, present an argument, or compare two approaches.
  - Easy (band 1-2): "The author's primary purpose in the passage is to..." with clearly stated purpose. Question stem: "Which choice best describes the overall purpose of the passage?"
  - Medium (band 3-5): "Which choice best describes how the passage is organized?" or "The third sentence serves primarily to..." Question stem: "The third sentence serves primarily to..."
  - Hard (band 6-7): questions requiring understanding of subtle rhetorical moves, like how an author qualifies a claim or transitions between perspectives. Question stem: "The author's discussion of X primarily serves to..."
- The question stem should reference a specific part of the passage (a sentence, paragraph, or the passage as a whole) to avoid ambiguity.
- Distractors should describe real functions the sentence/paragraph could serve, but not the one it primarily serves. For example, if the sentence provides evidence for a claim, a distractor might say "to introduce a counterargument."
- The passage should be 3-5 sentences with a clear organizational pattern.
