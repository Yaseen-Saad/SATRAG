- "Cross-Text Connections": Two short passages (70-120 words each) on the same topic (e.g., two scientists discussing a phenomenon, two historical documents, two reviews of the same book). The student must identify how the authors' perspectives relate — agreement, disagreement, shared assumption, or complementary viewpoints.
  - Easy (band 1-2): one author clearly agrees/disagrees with the other on a stated point. Question stem: "How does the author of Passage 2 respond to the claim in Passage 1?"
  - Medium (band 3-5): authors share a topic but emphasize different aspects or reach different conclusions. Question stem: "Which choice best describes the relationship between the two passages?"
  - Hard (band 6-7): the relationship is implicit — authors may agree on facts but disagree on implications, or one author may address a concern the other raises indirectly. Question stem: "The authors of both passages would most likely agree that..."
- CRITICAL FORMAT: In the "passage_text" field, include TWO passages separated by "--- Passage 1 ---" and "--- Passage 2 ---" headers. Each passage must be 70-150 words (aim for 100-120 words each). The passages should present DIFFERENT perspectives, arguments, or aspects of the same topic.
- The question stem must reference both passages (e.g., "How does the author of Passage 2 respond to the claim in Passage 1?").
- Common mistake: writing two passages that are too similar or that don't actually create a debatable relationship.
- Distractors should describe plausible but incorrect relationships (e.g., " Passage 1 disagrees with Passage 2" when they actually agree, or " Passage 2 provides evidence for Passage 1's claim" when Passage 2 critiques it).
