- "Lines, angles, and triangles": Use angle relationships (vertical, complementary, supplementary, parallel lines cut by transversals), triangle sum theorem, isosceles/equilateral triangles.
  - Easy (band 1-2): find complementary angle of 35°. Question stem: "What is the complement of a 35° angle?"
  - Medium (band 3-5): find x when parallel lines create algebraic angle expressions (e.g., "Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is 3x + 10 and its alternate interior angle is 5x - 20. Find x.")
  - Hard (band 6-7): multi-step problems combining triangle angle sums with parallel line theorems. Question stem: "In triangle ABC, angle A = 2x, angle B = 3x + 10, and angle C = x + 20. What is the value of x?"
- Wrong answers should test common mistakes: confusing complementary (sum to 90°) and supplementary (sum to 180°), using wrong angle pair relationships (alternate interior vs. corresponding), forgetting triangle angles sum to 180°, assuming lines are parallel without being told, confusing vertical angles with linear pairs.
- For SPR: numeric answer (angle measure or variable value). For MCQ: four numeric options.
