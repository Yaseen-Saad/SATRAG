- "Two-variable data: models and scatterplots": Interpret scatterplots, lines of best fit, correlation vs. causation. Easy: identify positive/negative correlation from a scatterplot. Medium: interpret slope of a line of best fit in context (e.g., "The line of best fit has equation y = 2.5x + 10, where x is hours studied and y is test score. What does the slope represent?"). Hard: interpolate or extrapolate, identify influential points, or distinguish correlation from causation (e.g., "Ice cream sales and drowning rates both increase in summer. Does this mean ice cream causes drowning?").
- Wrong answers should test common mistakes: confusing correlation with causation, extrapolating far beyond the data range, misinterpreting the y-intercept.
