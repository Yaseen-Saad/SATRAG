- "Two-variable data: models and scatterplots": Interpret scatterplots, lines of best fit, correlation vs. causation.
  - Easy (band 1-2): identify positive/negative correlation from a scatterplot description. Question stem: "A scatterplot shows that as hours of study increase, test scores also increase. What type of correlation is shown?"
  - Medium (band 3-5): interpret slope of a line of best fit in context (e.g., "The line of best fit has equation y = 2.5x + 10, where x is hours studied and y is test score. What does the slope represent?")
  - Hard (band 6-7): interpolate or extrapolate, identify influential points, or distinguish correlation from causation (e.g., "Ice cream sales and drowning rates both increase in summer. Does this mean ice cream causes drowning?")
- Wrong answers should test common mistakes: confusing correlation with causation (the #1 error), extrapolating far beyond the data range, misinterpreting the y-intercept (thinking it must be meaningful in context), confusing strong correlation with perfect prediction, thinking negative correlation means no relationship.
- For SPR: numeric answer (slope value, predicted value). For MCQ: four descriptive options.
