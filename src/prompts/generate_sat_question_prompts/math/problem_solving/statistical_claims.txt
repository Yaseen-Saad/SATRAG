- "Evaluating statistical claims: observational studies and experiments": Distinguish observational studies from experiments, identify confounding variables, understand random assignment and control groups.
  - Easy (band 1-2): identify whether a study is observational or experimental. Question stem: "Researchers measured the blood pressure of 500 volunteers and found that those who exercise regularly have lower blood pressure. Is this an observational study or a controlled experiment?"
  - Medium (band 3-5): determine which variable is the response variable, or identify the control group. Question stem: "In a study testing a new drug, one group receives the drug and another receives a placebo. What is the control group?"
  - Hard (band 6-7): identify confounding variables or flaws in study design (e.g., "A study finds that people who drink coffee have lower rates of heart disease. What is a possible confounding variable?")
- Wrong answers should test common mistakes: confusing correlation with causation in study contexts, misunderstanding random assignment (thinking it's the same as random selection), failing to identify confounders, thinking observational studies can prove causation, confusing statistical significance with practical significance.
- All questions are MCQ with four descriptive options.
